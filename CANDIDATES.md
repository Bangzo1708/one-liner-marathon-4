# One-Liner Marathon — round 4 candidates

Brief: one single-line comedy joke, up to 280 characters, judged head to
head by a deadpan stand-up-comic judge on: (1) works as a joke in one line,
(2) surprise that lands on first read, (3) economy, (4) distinct voice,
(5) holds up on a second read.

## Candidate A — noise-cancelling headphones
> I bought noise-cancelling headphones so I could hear myself think. Turns out I had nothing to say.
> (103 characters)

Strengths: clean setup-to-punchline turn, economical, self-deprecating voice
that holds up on a second read.
Weaknesses: "noise-cancelling headphones" is a well-worn joke topic; the
field is likely full of gadget jokes, so it risks sounding generic.

## Candidate B — grandmother's Wi-Fi password
> My grandmother left me her Wi-Fi password in her will — the only inheritance I've ever actually used.
> (105 characters)

Strengths: specific and warm, surprising turn (inheritance = a password),
distinct point of view, no programmer cliché.
Weaknesses: the punchline is gentle rather than laugh-out-loud; it reads
more sweet than funny, and "Wi-Fi password" jokes are common.

## Candidate C — silent retreat review (CHOSEN)
> I went to a silent retreat and left a one-star review. They never replied.
> (74 characters)

Strengths: the tightest of the three (74 chars — also helps the size
tie-break); the punchline turns the retreat's own rule back on itself, so
the joke lands on the first read and still works on the second; deadpan
voice matches the judge's persona; no cliché topic (no code, no AI, no
therapist, no "my wife").

## Selection
Chosen: **Candidate C**. It wins on every criterion in order: it is the
most clearly a one-line joke with a real punchline; its surprise is the
least predictable of the three; it is the most economical by a wide margin;
its voice is the most distinct (deadpan complaint vs. a silent retreat);
and it holds up on a second read because the punchline is a logical
consequence of the setup, not a one-off shock.
